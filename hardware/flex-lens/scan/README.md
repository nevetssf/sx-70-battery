# Scanning the Original

Do this **before** any further handling. The flex degrades with every touch, and a good scan is the only insurance against the original crumbling mid-project.

## Method

- **Flatbed scanner only.** A phone or camera introduces lens distortion and perspective error that will quietly scale your outline wrong.
- **2400 dpi optical** (not interpolated). 1200 is a usable floor if 2400 is not available.
- **Put a steel rule in the frame**, in the same plane as the flex, on every scan. This is the scale reference and it is not optional — scanner dpi is nominal and drifts between axes.
- Scan **both sides**. Include the outline and at least two through-features (holes, notches) visible from both, to register the two images.
- Save **lossless** — TIFF or PNG. Never JPEG; the compression artefacts blur trace edges exactly where you need them sharp.
- Support a curled flex flat with light pressure from the lid only. **Do not force a crumbling substrate flat** — scan it as it lies and correct later. A cracked original is worth less than an imperfect scan.

## Continuity map

Meter the original before storage and record net-by-net here, including which pads connect to the 7-way body interface. Cross-check against [OpenSX70 For Dummies part 2](https://opensx70.com/tutorials/opensx70-for-dummies-2/), but trust the meter.

## Storage

Flat, between parchment, in a rigid card sandwich, in a labelled bag. Minimise handling.
